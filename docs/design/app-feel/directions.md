# Widgie identity, round 2

The first round was rejected as "AI like". The follow-up correction changed the target. Widgie should not borrow the cozy pomodoro look, or Duolingo's. It needs one ownable idea of its own, carried through every screen. This round has three such ideas. Each grows out of what Widgie does: plain words become a small living object on your home screen.

| Direction | Core idea | File | Render |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Proofmarks | Your sentence is the blueprint, and Widgie marks it up like an editor. | `a-proofmarks.html` | `a-proofmarks.png` |
| B. Tileworks | Every widget is a glazed tile, and your home screen is a wall you tile. | `b-tileworks.html` | `b-tileworks.png` |
| C. The Caret | Widgie is the text cursor, come alive. | `c-caret.html` | `c-caret.png` |

Each page shows seven 390 pt phone frames. They are Library, Library empty, Describe, Building, Preview and refine, Saved, and Failure. The live widget faces are the real eval renders from `Widgie-p2/Engine/.artifacts/evals/p2-refine-2`.

The pages are generated by `gen/a.py`, `gen/b.py`, and `gen/c.py`, with shared geometry in `gen/common.py`. That module holds the hand-drawn brush, wobbly loops, arrows, and rough rectangles. `gen/shoot.sh` renders a page with headless Chrome. To rebuild one, run `python3 gen/a.py "$PWD/a-proofmarks.html" && gen/shoot.sh "$PWD/a-proofmarks.html" "$PWD/a-proofmarks.png" 1920 2130`.

The pages load New York and SF Pro straight from `/System/Library/Fonts`, so they render true only on a Mac. Elsewhere the browser falls back to serif and system fonts. Nanum Pen Script is the only Google Font, and only A uses it.

## What the references taught

Screenshots are saved in `refs/`. `refs/sheet-*.png` are contact sheets of each app's App Store screenshots, all taken from the US App Store pages fetched this session.

- **Cat On Chair** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6746562271)). One sentence is the whole product: "a cat appears on the chair keeping you company." The crayon texture covers everything, including the UI type. The timer digits are hand-drawn, and Start is a word with a hand-drawn ring around it. The chrome shrinks to four tiny corner words (Collect, Settings, Stats, Shop), so the drawing owns the screen. Even the App Store backdrop is the same green felt.
- **My Little Pomodoro** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6737629792)). A full-bleed lofi room, with the timer as a thin ring floating over it. The room grows as you focus, so the art is also the reward.
- **Pomocat** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1667838757)). Extreme reduction. One yellow, one black monoline cat, white UI. The cat face is the icon, and the stamp calendar uses the same face. Two colors and one line weight make it recognizable at a glance.
- **Focus Friend** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id6742278016)). One maroon ink does everything, from type and line art to icon and the lock screen. It adds emotional stakes: "If you interrupt your Bean by turning off the timer, they'll be really really sad."
- I did not review Finch, Forest, Pom: Kitty Timer, or Lofumi this session.

The shared lesson. None of these apps is cozy by accident. Each picked one idea that came from the product's own verb (focus means company, watching, growing). Then it carried that idea into the icon, the art, the type, the button shapes, the failure state, and the store page. Widgie's verb is "turn words into a thing on my home screen". All three directions start there. None reuses a reference's character, art, layout, or palette.

## A. Proofmarks

**Concept.** Your sentence is the source code. While Widgie builds, a red pencil circles the words it understood. Then it draws an arrow from each word to the part of the widget that word became, while a blue layout pencil sketches the frame. A refinement becomes an edit mark on your original sentence. Asking for a goal of 10 strikes through the 8 and writes 10 above it with a caret. The library is a list of marked-up sentences.

**Why it is ownable.** It is the only direction that shows the person how their words turned into UI. A non-coder sees cause and effect. The marks are also a visual signature no widget or AI app uses. Red pencil means editing, and non-repro blue means layout. Both are real print-shop conventions, so the system has a reason, not a style.

**Companion.** There is no creature. The hand holding the pencil is the personality. It circles eagerly, underlines twice when it is sure, puts a question mark where it failed, and writes "yes!" when you save.

**Palette.**

| Role | Hex |
| --- | --- |
| Ground (cobalt) | `#1F318A` |
| Raised surface | `#263B9C` |
| Hairline | `#3A4FAE` |
| Text | `#F1F3FF` (10.2:1) |
| Secondary text | `#AEB9EC` (5.9:1) |
| Red pencil, primary action | `#FF5A3C` |
| Label on red | `#0E1640` (5.6:1) |
| Blue layout pencil | `#86BDFF` (5.8:1) |

**Type.** New York for anything the person wrote, as `Font.system(.title, design: .serif)`. SF Pro for the app's own voice. Nanum Pen Script (OFL, bundled) appears only for the two or three handwritten notes, "10", "yes!", and "?". It could be replaced with vector lettering.

**Buttons.** Primary is 56 pt tall, full width inside 24 pt margins, with a 10 pt continuous corner radius. Fill `#FF5A3C`, label SF Pro Semibold 17 in `#0E1640`, and a 2 pt inner bottom shade at 14% black. Pressed drops the fill to `#E84B2F` and scales to 0.98. Secondary is a 1.5 pt `#6B7FD6` stroke with a white label. Disabled fills `#2A3E9E` with a `#7183CF` label. The "New widget" button leads with a hand-drawn insert caret.

**Animation moments.**
- Building runs the marks in order: a circle, an underline, a box, a wavy line. Each draws on in about 350 ms on an ease-out curve. Its arrow follows, then the matching wireframe part fills in. The marks track the real build steps.
- "Fixing a layout problem" draws a red box around the overflowing number and a move arrow.
- In the sample picker, the selection is a circle that redraws around the chosen word.
- On save, a big loop wraps the widget, then flick marks and "yes!" appear.
- On failure, the arrow from your change breaks halfway and ends in a "?".

**Haptics.** A light impact as each mark lands, capped at four per build. `.selection` in the picker, `.success` on save, `.error` on failure.

**Copy samples.** "One sentence in. One widget out." / "Each one started as a sentence." / "Done. I marked the change on your sentence." / "Saved. It's yours now." / "Check that the bridge is running. Your sentence is saved, so you can send it again."

**Engine dependency.** To draw honest arrows, the builder has to return which phrase produced which widget part, a span-to-element map. Without it the marks are decoration, so this direction is a product decision as well as a visual one.

## B. Tileworks

**Concept.** Widgets are small fired objects. You describe one, Widgie fires it in a brick kiln, and Save presses the tile into your wall with a puff of clay dust and a clack. The library is the wall, glazed tiles set tight in mortar. Example prompts are unfired bisque tiles, and inputs are pressed into the clay.

**Why it is ownable.** No widget app treats a widget as a physical thing. Rounded-square tiles on a grid already look like home-screen widgets, so the metaphor is native rather than borrowed. The terracotta ground with real surface relief is unlike any app category's default.

**Companion.** None. The kiln is the character. It glows and shimmers while it works, and it goes cold with a thin wisp when the bridge is down.

**Palette.**

| Role | Hex |
| --- | --- |
| Ground (terracotta) | `#A9482B` |
| Recess, grout | `#7E311C` |
| Text (slip inlay) | `#FBEBDD` (4.9:1) |
| Secondary text | `#FCE3D3` (4.7:1) |
| Cobalt glaze, primary action | `#2F4FA8` (label 6.4:1) |
| Cobalt edge | `#1E3474` |
| Bisque | `#E6B38D` |
| Bisque edge | `#B07B55` |
| Bisque label | `#6A2814` (5.8:1) |
| Honey glaze | `#E8A93E` |
| Celadon | `#8FC2A4` |
| Kiln glow | `#FF8A2B` |

Text colors are text only. The page ground is terracotta, never cream. The bisque appears only on small objects.

**Type.** SF Pro Expanded Heavy for the stamped headlines, as `.fontWidth(.expanded)` on iOS 16 and later. SF Pro for everything else.

**Buttons.** Primary is a cobalt glazed tile, 58 pt tall with a 16 pt radius. Its gradient runs `#4A6BCB` to `#2F4FA8` to `#284494`. A 5 pt `#1E3474` bottom edge gives the tile its thickness, and a soft gloss band sits across the top. Pressed moves the face down 4 pt as the edge shrinks to 1 pt, like a tile pushed into the wall. Secondary is a matte bisque tile with a 4 pt `#B07B55` edge. Disabled is an empty recessed slot.

**Animation moments.**
- The kiln glow breathes while it works, using a random 1.2 to 2 s flicker on the gradient's center stop. Heat shimmer rises above it.
- The tile inside brightens as each step completes.
- On save, the tile drops into its slot with a squash, dust puffs out from under the edges, and a few glaze chips fly.
- On failure, the kiln fades from glowing to cold over 600 ms.

**Haptics.** A rigid impact at intensity 0.8 for the clack when a tile sets. A soft impact as each step completes. `.warning` on failure.

**Copy samples.** "Your wall is bare." / "Unfired, waiting for an idea." / "In the kiln." / "Re-firing. The number ran past the edge." / "Set. Water is on your wall." / "The kiln is cold, so nothing changed."

## C. The Caret

**Concept.** Every app has a blinking bar that waits for you to type. In Widgie, that bar is the character. It listens while you type, gets up and draws your widget's outline with its tip, sits on the frame while you try samples, jumps when you save, and slumps at the gap of a broken bridge when the builder is unreachable. The app sits on a home-screen dot grid in one loud chartreuse with ink. Failure drains the world to grey.

**Why it is ownable.** Widgie's input is words, so its mascot is the thing you type into. Nobody else can claim that, and it is not a cat, a bean, or a blob. A bar with two eyes works as the app icon, the wordmark's last letter, the text cursor in every field, the speaker mark in the transcript, and the trailing bar on every primary button. One shape runs through the whole system. The chartreuse ground is unlike any category default and lets dark widgets pop hardest of the three.

**Companion personality.** Attentive and a little proud. It acts only through posture, because it has no limbs or mouth.

| Pose | When |
| --- | --- |
| Idle, blinking | It waits. |
| Leaning toward the text | You type. |
| Drawing, tilted like a pencil | It builds. |
| Sitting on a frame | You try samples. |
| Jumping, stretched, happy eyes | You save. |
| Slumped, half-lidded | The bridge is down. |

Its blink is the cursor blink.

**Palette.**

| Role | Hex |
| --- | --- |
| Ground (chartreuse) | `#D3EE3E` |
| Ink | `#151611` (13.9:1) |
| Secondary text (olive) | `#55601A` (5.2:1) |
| Grid dots | ink at 17% |
| Drained failure ground | `#C6C9B5` |
| Failure text | `#46493C` (5.5:1) |

**Type.** SF Pro only. Heavy for headlines at 42 to 64 pt with tight tracking (minus 1.4 to minus 2.2). Bold and Semibold for UI.

**Buttons.** Primary is an ink slab, 60 pt tall with a 14 pt radius. Its label is SF Pro Bold 18 in chartreuse, followed by a 3 by 22 pt chartreuse bar that blinks, so every primary action reads as "type here, go". Pressed stops the blink and nudges the slab 2 pt down. Secondary is a 2 pt ink outline. Disabled is ink at 12% with the label at 38%. Refine suggestions are 38 pt outlined chips with a 10 pt radius, never capsules.

**Animation moments.**
- The cursor blink runs 530 ms on and 530 ms off, matching the system caret.
- While building, the outline draws clockwise and the caret rides the end of the path. When a part is understood, it fills from dashed to solid.
- When you send a refine, the caret in the composer hops up into the transcript.
- On save, it crouches, jumps 30% of its height with a stretch, lands with a squash, and its eyes turn to arcs.
- On failure, the background drains from chartreuse to grey over 500 ms, and the caret bends over the gap.

**Haptics.** A light impact when each step completes. A heavy impact on the jump's landing, then `.success`. `.error` on the slump. Typing gets no haptics.

**Copy samples.** The voice is first person, short, and plain. "Tell me a widget." / "Say it in plain words. I'll build it while you watch, in about 40 seconds." / "I'll match its look." / "Saved." / "Check that the bridge is running. Nothing changed."

## Shipping the art in SwiftUI

- **A.**
  - Port `brush()` from `gen/common.py`, about 80 lines, to a `Shape`. It turns a centerline into a filled outline that tapers and varies with pressure. Seed it from the phrase so the same sentence always gets the same marks.
  - For the draw-on animation, mask the filled shape with the centerline's `trim(from: 0, to: p)` stroked wide.
  - For pencil grain, mask with a tiled 256 px noise PNG, or use an iOS 17 `colorEffect` shader.
  - To place marks on words, iOS 18's `TextRenderer` exposes `Text.Layout` runs. Tag each phrase with a custom `TextAttribute` to get its bounds. On iOS 17, fall back to `UITextView` with `boundingRect(forGlyphRange:)`.
- **B.**
  - Ship the clay relief as a seamless 512 px PNG tiled with `resizingMode: .tile`, or as a noise-and-lighting shader.
  - A tile is the widget snapshot clipped to `RoundedRectangle(cornerRadius: 22, style: .continuous)`, with gloss and inner-shadow gradient overlays. A darker copy offset 5 pt underneath gives the thickness.
  - The kiln bricks are one vector PDF in the asset catalog. The glow is an animated `RadialGradient` behind the arch mask, using `phaseAnimator` for the flicker.
  - The dust is a `Canvas` driven by `TimelineView`, or a `KeyframeAnimator`.
- **C.**
  - The whole character is one `Shape` whose four Bezier control points are its `animatableData`. Each pose is a control-point set, so `withAnimation(.spring(duration: 0.35, bounce: 0.3))` morphs between any two poses.
  - The eyes are two small capsules placed at the top end along the curve's tangent. Blinking squashes their height.
  - While building, a `RoundedRectangle` is trimmed to `p`. The caret sits at `path.trimmedPath(from: 0, to: p).currentPoint`, tilted to the tangent.
  - The dot grid is one `Canvas`. Failure animates the background color and applies `.saturation(0)` to the illustration.
  - It needs no art assets at all.

All three use `.sensoryFeedback` (iOS 17) for haptics and respect Reduce Motion. With Reduce Motion on, draw-ons become fades, the kiln stops flickering, and the caret stops bouncing.

## Ranking

1. **C. The Caret.** It is the most ownable. The character is the product's input, so no competitor can share it. It carries through every surface at no extra cost, from icon and wordmark to cursor, buttons, and transcript. It is also the cheapest to build and maintain: one animatable `Shape`, no illustration pipeline, and one typeface. It passes the "AI template" test because it commits hard to one color, one shape, and one grid. The risks are that a full chartreuse ground may tire some people over long sessions, and that a reviewer may call it loud. A dark-mode variant (ink ground, chartreuse caret) is the hedge. I would test that before committing.
2. **A. Proofmarks.** It has the deepest idea. It teaches a non-coder how their words became a widget, and it turns refinement into visible edits. Two costs keep it below C. It needs an engine change to return a phrase-to-part map, or the arrows are fiction. And mark layout over live text is the hardest engineering of the three. If the team wants Widgie to feel smart rather than playful, pick A. A good graft is to let C's caret underline the understood words during Building.
3. **B. Tileworks.** It is the most tactile and the warmest, and the kiln is the strongest single illustration. But it says the least about words, which is Widgie's actual verb. It also has the heaviest asset and texture pipeline to keep looking good across devices and dark mode. Its warm materials sit closest to the cozy genre the user said to avoid.

## What I checked

- Every page was rendered with headless Chrome and inspected at 1x and 2x (crops in `crops/`).
- Fixes after inspection:
  - A's navy ground read as stock dark mode, so it moved to cobalt.
  - In A, the inserted "10" broke the line wrap.
  - In A, the picker's circle sat on the wrong option.
  - In A, the failure arrow's mark landed on the "kept" label.
  - In A, the Building arrows started in empty space.
  - B's speck texture had an inverted threshold that buried every bisque label.
  - B's library did not read as a wall until the mortar panel was added.
  - B's dust looked like bubbles.
  - In C, the small caret read as a cable between widgets in the library.
  - In C, the caret covered the "2/8" while drawing in Building.
  - C's suggestion chips were crowded.
- Text contrast was computed for every text and ground pair. Three pairs were below 4.5:1 and were fixed: A's button label, B's secondary text, and C's failure text. The ratios above are measured.
- All widget faces are real engine output. The wireframes, kiln, and characters are generated SVG.

## Round 3. The Caret, simplified, in three palettes

The user picked The Caret but disliked the chartreuse, and found it too wordy and complicated for a first-time user. The character and its poses stay. Each screen now has one action and very little text, and the caret is drawn larger so its posture carries the meaning. The seven screens are identical across the three palettes, and all three come from one generator, `gen/c2.py <palette> <out.html>`.

### Text removed

- **Library.** Removed the "Your widgets" title. What's left is the small wordmark, the widgets, and "New widget". The caret leans in from the top corner.
- **Library, empty.** Removed the explanation line. What's left is a large caret and "Tell me a widget."
- **Describe.** Removed the "New widget" nav title, the "What should it do?" heading, and the "Or try one of these" label. The examples went from full sentences to "Water tracker", "Word of the day", and "To-do list". The photo card's title and caption became an icon button. The caret now stands at 58 pt as the cursor inside the field.
- **Building.** Removed the echoed sentence, "About 40 seconds", the three-row checklist, and its explanation line. What's left is the caret drawing the outline at 150 pt tall, one status line that changes, and a small text Cancel.
- **Preview.**
  - Removed the "Water" nav title.
  - The "Try it with" label and three-option list became one "Morning" menu control.
  - The full transcript became the latest exchange only, "Make the goal 10." and "Done."
  - There are two suggestion chips instead of three.
  - The caret watches the widget from the free column.
- **Saved.** The two-sentence instruction became "Press and hold your home screen, then tap Edit to add it." Removed "Make another".
- **Failure.** Removed "Check that the bridge is running. Nothing changed.", the "Last working version" label, and "Keep this version". What's left is the slumped caret at the broken bridge, "Couldn't reach the widget builder.", the kept widget, and "Try again". This drops the hint about the bridge. If that hint still matters during development, it could go behind a small info button.

### Pick

**Sky.** It reads the calmest and most clearly to a first-time user. It gives the dark widget faces the most contrast (10.8:1), so they look like what they are. The ink caret is the strongest figure on its ground. And it moves furthest from the loudness the user rejected in chartreuse. Coral is the playful runner-up. Its secondary text sits at 4.8:1, just above the bar. Night is the most atmospheric, and the marigold caret is lovely, but the widgets need an outline to separate from the ground. Night would make a good dark-mode counterpart to Sky, using the same caret in marigold.
